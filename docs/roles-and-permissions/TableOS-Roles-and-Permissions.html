1. Summary in plain words

TableOS copies Airtable's three-level model: Organization → Workspace → Base. A role given on a workspace applies to every base in it; a role given on a base applies to that base only. Inside a base there are four roles: Creator, Editor, Commenter, Viewer. On top of roles there are field permissions (who may edit a field's values) and device approval (a member's new browser must be approved by the owner).

The part that causes most confusion: every person who signs up automatically gets their own organization, in which they are the Owner, with a workspace called "Home". That is also true for people you invite. So an invited member is, at the same time, a limited member of your organization and the Owner of their own organization. Their Home page mixes both, and in their own organization they can create workspaces and bases freely.

Most important problems found
  1. Anyone with access to one of your bases — even a Viewer — can copy the entire base, with all its records, into their own workspace and becomes Creator of the copy. tested
  2. Everybody can create workspaces, because everybody is the Owner of the personal organization created at sign-up. tested
  3. Every role, including Viewer, can download every table as CSV/Excel; there is no setting to restrict export. tested
  4. Only one person (the organization Owner) can manage people; the Admin role exists in the database but cannot be assigned, and ownership cannot be transferred.
  5. Sign-up is open to anyone who reaches the site, and every sign-up creates a new organization.

Section 10 lists all 20 gaps with evidence. Section 11 proposes an Airtable-aligned model with clear settings (for example "Who can create workspaces: Owners and Admins only") and a three-phase plan, plus the decisions we need from you.

3. How accounts, organizations and workspaces come into being today

3.1 Sign-up

Signing up (POST /v1/auth/signup, public) does all of this in one step (apps/server/src/modules/auth/routes.ts lines 173–221):

  1. creates the account;
  2. creates a new organization named "<Name>'s workspace" of kind personal;
  3. makes the person its Owner;
  4. creates a workspace Home and gives the person the workspace Owner role on it;
  5. starts a free plan for that organization.

3.2 Being invited

When the Owner of organization A invites someone, the invitation is for a workspace (all its bases) or for selected bases. Accepting it (POST /v1/invitations/accept) adds the person to organization A with the organization role member and creates the workspace or base role. The invited person must sign in (or sign up) with the invited email address — and if they sign up, they also get their own personal organization from step 3.1.

Arslan signs up
└── Organization "Arslan's workspace"     Arslan = Owner
    └── Workspace "Home"                   Arslan = workspace Owner
        └── Base "Signize.us"

Arslan invites Mahad to the base "Signize.us" as Editor.
Mahad signs up and accepts:
├── Organization "Mahad's workspace"     Mahad = Owner      (created by his sign-up)
│   └── Workspace "Home"                   Mahad = workspace Owner
│       └── (anything Mahad creates here is outside Arslan's control)
└── Organization "Arslan's workspace"    Mahad = member
    └── Workspace "Home"                   (visible to Mahad, showing only Signize.us)
        └── Base "Signize.us"              Mahad = Editor
On Mahad's Home page both organizations' workspaces appear together, both called "Home". The + Create workspace button creates the workspace in his own organization (the first one he owns, workspace/routes.ts lines 80–99). tested — an invited Viewer had two organizations (own: Owner, company: member), two "Home" workspaces, and created a new workspace (HTTP 201).

3.3 Creating workspaces and bases

  • Workspace: the server allows it to anyone who is Owner of an organization, and puts it in the first organization they own. Because every account owns its personal organization, in practice everyone can create workspaces. The creator becomes workspace Owner.
  • Base: needs Owner or Creator on the workspace (or organization Owner/Admin). The creator becomes base Creator.

4.3 Base roles

Creator, Editor, Commenter, Viewer (see the matrix in section 7) plus:

  • No access to this base (stored as role none, since 0.11.0): blocks one base that a workspace role would otherwise open.
  • Interface only: defined (read records and views through an interface) but not offered anywhere yet.

4.4 Field permissions (per field)

Field header → Edit field permissions (needs Creator). Levels: Editors and up (default) · Creators and up · Specific people · Nobody. They only limit editing values; everyone who can open the base still sees the field. "Nobody" also blocks the Owner and Creators (schema/field-permissions.ts line 35). Automations and restores are not limited by field permissions.

4.5 Time limits and status

  • Access until: any workspace or base role can end automatically on a date.
  • Suspend / Reactivate: blocks everything immediately, keeps the roles.
  • Remove: deletes all roles and devices in the organization (the person's own personal organization is untouched).

5. How TableOS decides what someone can do

For every request on a base, the server computes one effective base role (packages/permissions/src/snapshot.ts, apps/server/src/modules/access/compile.ts):

  1. The browser's device must not be blocked in that organization (device approval) — otherwise the organization is invisible.
  2. The person must be an active member of the organization — otherwise no access at all.
  3. Organization Owner or Admin → treated as workspace Owner → Creator.
  4. Workspace Owner → Creator, whatever the base says.
  5. Otherwise, if the base has its own setting, it wins (higher, lower, or No access).
  6. Otherwise the base inherits the workspace role.
  7. Expired roles count as no role. No role = no access (the base is not listed and returns "not found").

The effective role is then checked against the action the request needs (for example record.update), and field permissions / synced-field rules are applied on writes.

7.6 Share links and forms

  • Create, change, regenerate, revoke share links: Editor and Creator. List links: anyone with base access. tested (Viewer: refused, 403).
  • Link types: view (read that view), base (read the whole base), form (submit only, including file uploads). Visitors never edit.
  • Options: public or password; expiry date; "allow copy" (display hint only — not enforced by the server).
  • Wrong passwords: 5 tries → 15-minute lock for that visitor, and the organization Owner is alerted (email + IP).
  • Form submissions are created as "Form" (no account needed), count toward the plan's record limit and are rate-limited.

7.7 Interfaces

  • Build (create, edit, publish, pages): base Creator. Use published interfaces: any base role (non-builders only see published ones). There is no "interface-only" access yet — to use an interface a person needs a role on the base.

7.8 Automations

ActionWho
See automations and their run historyAny base role
Create, edit, duplicate, delete, test, get webhook URLCreator
Press an automation button in a recordEditor and Creator
Incoming webhook triggerAnyone with the secret webhook URL
Whose permissions and connected accounts (Gmail, Slack, …) the actions useThe person who last saved the automation (otherwise its creator). If that person loses access, the actions fail.

7.9 Synced tables and connectors

  • Connections (Gmail, Slack, HubSpot, Airtable) belong to the person who connected them, not to the organization. Others can't use or see someone else's connection.
  • Create a synced table: base Creator, using their own connection (or, for a table from another base, read access to that base in the same organization).
  • Sync now / Stop: Editor and Creator. Pause, resume, two-way, "keep as text", stop syncing: Creator. Two-way can only be turned on by the person who set up the sync.
  • Synced fields are read-only for everyone (except fields made writable by two-way HubSpot sync).

7.10 Attachments, import, export

  • Upload: Editor and Creator. Download: anyone with base access. File links in records are signed and expire.
  • Import: Editor and Creator. Export (CSV/Excel): every role, including Viewer (G4).

7.11 Search, notifications, contacts

  • Search only returns bases the person can open.
  • Notifications are personal; they are not re-checked when someone loses access to a base (G17).
  • The workspace contacts directory only requires organization membership (G9).

7.12 API tokens

  • Only the organization Owner creates them. Scopes: read, write, delete; optionally limited to chosen bases; optional expiry.
  • A token can reach only the records API and schema reads (access/api-tokens.ts requiredScope); everything else is refused.
  • A token works only while it is unrevoked, unexpired and its creator is still an active Owner of the organization. It acts with the Owner's access.

8. Security controls

ControlHow it works today
Device approvalOn by default per organization. A member's new browser is "Waiting" until the Owner approves it; until then that organization's data is invisible to them. Revoked devices are blocked even if approval is turned off. The Owner gets a bell notification and has a per-person log (devices, active/ended sessions, approvals and revocations). Owners never need approval.
Wrong passwords (sign-in)Warning from the 3rd try, 15-minute lock after 5 wrong passwords; the organization Owner is alerted with the email and IP. The Owner's own account is never locked (it is still rate-limited: 10 tries per 15 minutes per IP).
Wrong passwords (share links)Same policy per visitor IP; Owner alerted.
SessionsNever expire (since 0.8.1): you stay signed in until you sign out, change your password, or the Owner removes or suspends you or revokes the device. Each person can sign out other sessions.
Two-factor (MFA)Optional per person (authenticator app). Not enforceable by the organization.
Audit logSign-ins, failed sign-ins, invitations, role changes, device decisions, share links, API tokens and sync settings are recorded (not yet viewable in the app except the device log).

11. Recommended model and decisions needed

11.1 Target model (Airtable-aligned, organization-centric)

Organization (your company — the only place company data lives)
├── Owners        one or more; transferable; everything incl. billing
├── Admins        manage people, devices, workspaces, policies (no billing)
├── Billing admin plan and invoices only
├── Members       get access only through workspace / base roles
└── Guests        base-only people; can never create workspaces or bases

Organization policies (new settings, defaults in brackets)
  • Who can create workspaces ............. [Owners and Admins]
  • Who can invite people ................. [Owners and Admins]  (+ workspace Owners, + base Creators)
  • Who can export data (CSV/Excel/API) ... [Creators and up]
  • Public share links .................... [Allowed, password required]
  • Copy bases outside the organization ... [Never]
  • Require two-factor ..................... [Off]
  • Session length ......................... [30 days]
  • Sign-up ................................ [Invite only]  (or allowed email domains)

Workspace roles:  Owner (assignable, can manage that workspace's people) · Creator · Editor · Commenter · Viewer
Base roles:       Creator · Editor · Commenter · Viewer · Interface only · No access
